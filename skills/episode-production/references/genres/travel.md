# Travel exploration and fictional-world documentary

Let viewers discover the world with a traveller who has a current goal. Start with
an established anomaly, spectacle or concrete contradiction, then explain what the
traveller is trying to confirm. When the brief requests a world-introduction card,
use its exact position and text. A cold open needs an intelligible time return and
does not require replaying the complete event later.

Alternate selfie sharing, POV/eyeline observation, purposeful action, readable
details and reactions according to new information. Environment shots establish
location, an obstacle, scale or a clue. Preserve realistic body/equipment scale;
use an existing person or landmark to convey a spectacle's size. A discovery
changes the next action or question. Keep uncertain explanations uncertain.

Vary pace with action and recognition. Let short reactions have weight, compress
repeated explanations, and leave useful looking/listening time. Voice is a current
observation or decision, not a lecture over every image. Use the author's natural
manner, clause-specific performance, action sounds and music that follows events.
The current task's audio-reference policy and subtitle method remain authoritative.

Carry equipment, prop holders, route, knowledge and time through each move. Preserve
the package's known events, threats and ending; add neither attacks nor revelations
to simulate stronger stakes. Next-destination options explain what is interesting
and what the traveller can actually do there, without claiming a vote or visiting
a destination before the audience selects it.

These principles draw on the project's travel revision and the observed visual
organization of the Giant Garden reference. That reference's audio was not heard
by the reviewing model; its music or vocal quality is not established by screenshots.
